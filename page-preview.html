The True Cost of Renting an Office Across Britain

London is still the most expensive place in Britain to rent an office. But in cities like Bristol and Leeds, office occupancy is now higher than in London, good space is getting harder to find, and prices have climbed sharply since 2021.

Desk prices in Milton Keynes and Leeds are up by more than a third since 2021, and Bristol and Milton Keynes have reached record highs.

FreeOfficeFinder analysed more than 2,000 office buildings listed on its platform, alongside more than ten years of its own price records. Here's where office rental prices are rising, why, and how prices compare across 19 towns and cities and 61 London areas.

Key findings

  1. Milton Keynes desk prices are up 44% since 2021, the biggest rise in our top five.
  2. Bristol and Milton Keynes are at record highs. Bristol's desk prices rose 19% in the last year alone.
  3. Office occupancy is higher in Bristol and Leeds than in London: 69% and 65% in early 2026, against 42% in London.
  4. A small office in London costs twice as much as in a typical UK city: £1,000 a month, against £500.
  5. A desk in Mayfair costs more than four times as much as one in Enfield: £971 a month, against £215.
  6. Liverpool has the cheapest coworking: £145 a desk a month, less than half London's £297.

How we did this

  • Desk prices: the monthly price of one desk in a private office, from FreeOfficeFinder's price records. We compare 2021 with 2026.
  • Prices today: the starting price of the smallest private office (for two people) and of a coworking desk, from more than 2,000 offices listed on FreeOfficeFinder in October 2026.
  • Outside checks: we compared our trends with government figures on office rental values (used to set business rates) and figures from property agents.

Why office rental prices are rising in cities like Bristol and Leeds

Busier offices

Bristol and Leeds have the highest office occupancy of the big UK cities, well ahead of London.

Better, not bigger

With hybrid working, firms want better offices, not bigger ones. In 2025, almost four in five established firms that moved office in the big regional cities and the South East chose prime offices, and more than twice as many firms expanded as downsized.

Good offices are running out

Just 3% of prime office space in the big regional cities is empty.

Almost nothing new is being built

The big regional cities will finish only about a third as much new office space in 2026 as in a typical year between 2020 and 2024.

We looked for cities where desk prices rose faster than the UK average, and where government and property agents' figures show rents rising too. Five cities made the cut.

Newcastle

+27% since 2021, from £156 to £199 a month

What's driving demand: government jobs. The DWP and HMRC are moving into major new offices in the city in 2027, including the huge Pilgrims Quarter, and just 3% of prime office space is empty. Desk prices have recovered from a sharp fall in 2019 and 2020.

Brighton

+26% since 2021, from £275 to £345 a month

What's driving demand: a fast-growing economy that's short of prime office space. Brighton's economy has grown twice as fast as the national average since 2013, but less than a third of its empty office space is prime. In January, Capgemini signed the area's biggest office deal in nearly three years.

In Milton Keynes, Leeds and Bristol, desk prices have risen faster than inflation.

How we got here

  • 2020–21: offices empty out. In April 2020, almost half of people in work did some of their work from home.
  • 2022–25: hybrid work, then back to the office. By 2025, more than a quarter of working adults were hybrid, and most civil servants are now expected in the office at least 60% of the time.
  • 2026: a scramble for the best space. Office attendance hit its highest level since the pandemic in February, and most of the office space taken in the regions this year was prime.

Costs are still climbing. Business rates in England and Wales are now worked out from office rental values 14.5% higher than in 2021. Rent growth is expected to slow in 2026, though prime rents in Birmingham and Manchester are still forecast to rise by double digits.

How does your town or city rank?

London is the most expensive place to rent an office, at £1,000 a month for the smallest private office. Outside London, the most expensive is Reading (£664), followed by Brighton (£600), then Oxford and Manchester (both £570). The cheapest is Basingstoke (£287), followed by Aberdeen (£360) and Cardiff (£380).

London: still Britain's most expensive place to rent an office

A small office in London costs £1,000 a month, double a typical UK city, and a coworking desk costs £297, the most in our study. The best addresses cost far more: prime rents in Mayfair and St James's are almost four times the highest outside London.

What's happening in London

  • Lower occupancy. London's offices are far less full than those in Bristol and Leeds.
  • Outer London rose fastest. Official rental values rose 18.6% in outer London between 2021 and 2024, against 14.0% in inner London. Harrow saw the biggest rise, but it is still the second-cheapest London area for a desk.
  • AI firms are moving into King's Cross. Google DeepMind, OpenAI and Meta are all based there, and AI companies took a third of the offices let to London tech firms in 2025, up from just 4% in 2015.
  • Record empty space in Docklands. Office vacancy in Docklands hit a record of almost 19% in early 2025, as HSBC and others announced plans to leave.
  • Less is being built. Building work started on about a third less London office space in 2025 than the year before.

Methodology

Terms we use
  • Desk prices: the average monthly price of one desk in a private office, from FreeOfficeFinder's historical pricing tool (quarterly since 2015).
  • Smallest private office: the median advertised starting price for listings whose smallest office is for two people. This is the price for the whole office, not per person. On this page we call it a "small office".
  • Coworking desk: the median advertised starting price for one desk.
  • Office occupancy: how full offices are on an average day compared with their capacity, from Remit Consulting, based on building entry data. This is different from vacancy (how much office space is empty and available to rent).
  • Prime office space: the best-quality offices in a city, often called Grade A.
  • Official rental values: the government's valuation of what offices would rent for, used to set business rates.
  • Prime rents: what the best offices in a city rent for per sq ft on traditional leases. These are a different measure from FreeOfficeFinder's monthly prices. Prime rents, and figures on empty offices and lettings, come from the property agents listed under Sources.
Desk prices
  • Change since 2021 compares October 2020–September 2021 with October 2025–September 2026. "Last year" compares October 2024–September 2025 with October 2025–September 2026.
  • "Record high" means the highest 12-month average since 2015. Comparisons with a peak or an earlier year also use 12-month averages.
  • Top 5: we selected cities where desk prices rose more than the tool's UK average (21%), official rental values rose more than the England and Wales figure (14.5%), and prime rents rose too. We ranked them by the rise in desk prices.
  • Prime rent periods: Knight Frank, end 2021 to end 2025, for Leeds, Bristol and Newcastle; Knight Frank, early 2022 to end 2025, for Brighton; Bidwells, spring 2022 to 2025, for Milton Keynes.
  • On this page, five-year desk price changes are shown only for the top 5, where outside figures also show rents rising. Changes for all 19 places are in the accompanying data.
  • Desk prices are not adjusted for inflation. Consumer prices rose about 28% over the same period (ONS, to August 2026, the latest available). Desk prices rose faster than inflation in Milton Keynes, Leeds and Bristol, and slightly slower in Newcastle and Brighton.
Prices today and rankings
  • Source: FreeOfficeFinder listings, collected 11 October 2026: 2,047 office buildings, of which 1,861 are in the 19 towns and cities we cover.
  • These are advertised starting prices for ready-to-use offices, not per-square-foot rents, and may exclude VAT or extra services.
  • We rank 19 places for small offices and 14 for coworking, using their FreeOfficeFinder listings. Each place covers the listings FreeOfficeFinder files under that name.
  • We excluded 11 listings priced under £50 or over £3,000 per desk a month as likely errors. Only one, a £30 coworking desk in Liverpool, affected any figure here.
  • Rank 1 is the most expensive. Where two places cost the same, the one with the pricier coworking desk ranks higher.
  • A "typical UK city" is the median price across the places we cover outside London. Yearly costs are monthly prices × 12.
  • Regions follow the official UK regions, with the South East and East shown together.
Official figures
  • England and Wales: Valuation Office Agency, 2026 business rates revaluation. Change in office rental values, April 2021 to April 2024, including inner and outer London (official ONS definitions) and London boroughs.
  • Scotland: Scottish Government, Non-domestic rates revaluation 2026 statistics. Change in office rateable values between the 2023 and 2026 revaluations (values as at April 2022 and April 2025). Shown alongside, not combined with, England and Wales.
  • Business numbers: ONS, UK business: activity, size and location 2026. Change in VAT and/or PAYE-registered businesses, March 2025 to March 2026.
  • These cover slightly different periods from our figures, so we use them to check the direction of our trends, not to combine with them.
  • Labels on city cards: "rising fast" means official rental values rose 20% or more; "rising" 10% to under 20%; "steady" 0% to under 10%; "falling" a fall.
London
  • Central London means the 41 areas FreeOfficeFinder lists as central London; "elsewhere in London" is all other London listings.
  • London figures exclude 12 listings that FreeOfficeFinder files under London but which are just outside Greater London (in Borehamwood, Elstree, Epsom, Brentwood, East Horsley and Ashford).
  • London area rankings use desk prices, October 2025–September 2026, across 61 areas (including Watford and Borehamwood, which the tool groups with London).
Rounding and samples
  • FreeOfficeFinder prices are rounded to the nearest pound and changes to the nearest whole per cent, halves rounded up. Changes are worked out before rounding. Official and third-party figures are shown as published.
  • Some prices are based on only a few listings, so treat them as a guide: small offices in Slough (5), Basingstoke (6), St Albans (6) and Milton Keynes (7); coworking in Cambridge (5), Oxford (5), Brighton (6), Milton Keynes (6) and Newcastle (7).
