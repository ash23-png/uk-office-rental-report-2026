The True Cost of Renting an Office Across Britain

Central London is still the most expensive place in Britain to rent an office, but costs are changing fast elsewhere too.

In cities like Bristol and Leeds, office occupancy is now higher than in London. Good space is becoming harder to find, and prices have climbed sharply since 2021.

Desk prices in Milton Keynes are also up by more than a third since 2021, with St Albans reaching record highs alongside Bristol.

This report looks at where office rental prices are rising, why, and how they compare across 61 areas of London and 19 towns and cities in the UK.

Key Findings

  1. Milton Keynes desk prices are up 39% since 2021, the biggest rise in our top seven.
  2. Bristol and St Albans are at record highs, with Bristol's desk prices up 16% in the last year alone.
  3. Office occupancy is higher in Bristol and Leeds, reaching 69% and 65% in early 2026, compared with 42% in London.
  4. A desk in central London costs £634 a month, double the UK average of £316.
  5. A desk in Mayfair costs more than four times as much as one in Enfield, at £1,006 a month, against £218.
  6. At £103 per desk per month, Newcastle is the cheapest place for coworking, just over a third of the price in London (£286).

What's Driving Demand for Offices in Cities like Bristol and Leeds

Busier offices

Bristol and Leeds have the highest office occupancy of the major UK cities, and both are well ahead of London. In 2025, more than twice as many established firms moving offices in the major regional cities and the South East expanded as downsized.

Better, not bigger

Hybrid working has led businesses to choose better offices rather than bigger ones. Of the companies that moved in 2025, almost four in five chose high-quality office space.

Good offices are running out

Just 3% of premium office space in the largest regional cities is empty.

Almost nothing new is being built

In 2026, these cities are expected to complete only about a third of the new office space they typically delivered each year between 2020 and 2024.

To find the top seven, we looked for cities where desk prices rose faster than the UK average (17%), and where official government figures also showed office rents rising faster than average.

The Top 7 Cities for Rising Office Rental Prices

1

Milton Keynes

+39% since 2021, from £221 to £308 a month

Milton Keynes is one of the UK's fastest-growing cities, yet no major new office developments are being built. Between 2011 and 2021, its population grew at more than twice the average rate for England, and around a third of the city's office demand now comes from businesses elsewhere.

2

Liverpool

+34% since 2021, from £196 to £262 a month

Business numbers in Liverpool grew 3.5% over the past year, and office lettings in early 2026 were up two-thirds year on year. Official office rental values rose 18% between 2021 and 2024, faster than the England and Wales average.

3

Leeds

+30% since 2021, from £264 to £343 a month

Leeds is a busy finance hub that is short of good space. Rents for premium offices rose 31% in the past year, the fastest of the UK's major regional cities, and the Bank of England is also opening an office in the area. Desk prices dipped in the last 12 months but remain well above 2021 levels.

4

St Albans Record high

+30% since 2021, from £253 to £328 a month

Costa Coffee is moving its UK headquarters to the city, and official office rental values rose 23% between 2021 and 2024, one of the biggest rises in our study.

5

Newcastle

+29% since 2021, from £162 to £209 a month

Demand in Newcastle is being driven by two government departments, the DWP and HMRC, which are moving into new offices in the city in 2027, including Pilgrims Quarter. Just 3% of premium office space is empty, and desk prices are rising again after a sharp fall in 2019 and 2020.

6

Bristol Record high

+28% since 2021, from £291 to £372 a month, up 16% in the last year

The AI boom is helping to drive demand in Bristol. Isambard-AI, the UK's most powerful AI supercomputer, opened nearby in 2025, and chip designer Graphcore signed the city's largest office deal of the first half of 2026. Bristol also has the highest office occupancy of the major UK cities, with less than 1% of its premium office space empty.

7

Brighton

+27% since 2021, from £271 to £344 a month

Brighton's economy has grown twice as fast as the national average since 2013, yet less than a third of its empty office space is considered premium. In January, Capgemini signed the area's biggest office deal in nearly three years.

How We Got Here

  • 2020 to 2021: offices empty as remote working rises: In April 2020, almost half of the working population did some of their work from home.
  • 2022 to 2025: hybrid working becomes established: By 2025, more than a quarter of working adults had hybrid arrangements, while most civil servants were expected in the office at least 60% of the time.
  • 2026: demand for the best space increases: Office attendance hit its highest level since the pandemic in February 2026, and most of the office space taken in the top regional cities this year was considered premium.

Property costs continue to rise: Business rates in England and Wales are now set using office rental values 14.5% higher than in 2021.

Rent growth is expected to slow in the remainder of 2026, although rents in Birmingham and Manchester for high-quality workspace are still forecast to rise by double digits.

Central London: Still Britain's Most Expensive Place to Rent an Office

A desk in central London costs £634 a month, double the UK average, and a coworking desk there costs £326, more than anywhere outside the capital.

At the top end, rents for premium offices in Mayfair and St James's are almost four times the highest found outside of London.

What's Happening in London

  • Lower occupancy. Office occupancy in London is much lower than in Bristol and Leeds.
  • Faster growth in outer London. Office rental values rose 18.6% in outer London between 2021 and 2024, against 14% in inner London. Harrow saw the biggest increase, yet it remains London's second-cheapest area for a desk.
  • AI firms are moving into King's Cross. In 2025, AI companies accounted for a third of all office space taken by London tech firms, up from just 4% in 2015. Google DeepMind, OpenAI and Meta are also all based in the area.
  • Record empty space in Docklands. Office vacancy in the Docklands hit a record of almost 19% in early 2025, as HSBC and others announced plans to leave the area.
  • Fewer new office developments. Construction began on about a third less new office space in London in 2025 than in 2024.

Methodology

All prices in this report come from FreeOfficeFinder's pricing tool, so every figure can be checked there. The tool is updated every quarter and uses prices from typically five business centres in each area to work out an average desk rate; private office prices are based on a three-desk workspace. Current prices are the tool's Q3 2026 figures (dated 16 July 2026). We checked the results against official government figures.

  • Desk price: the monthly price of one desk, in a private office or in coworking space, in Q3 2026 (the latest figures).
  • UK average: the pricing tool's average across our 19 towns and cities (£316 a desk).
  • Price changes: Q3 2021 (July) compared with Q3 2026 (July), as shown in the pricing tool. Prices are not adjusted for inflation.
  • Top seven: cities where desk prices rose faster than the UK average, and official office rental values rose faster than the England and Wales average.
  • Office occupancy: how full offices are on an average day. This is not the same as how much space is empty.
  • Small samples: where we list fewer than five buildings in an area, the tool uses pricing from other known centres, so treat these as a guide.

Where Our Data Comes From

  • FreeOfficeFinder (our data): pricing tool, desk prices by quarter (2015 to 2026)
  • Valuation Office Agency: 2026 business rates revaluation (England and Wales)
  • Scottish Government: Non-domestic rates revaluation 2026 statistics
  • Office for National Statistics: UK business: activity, size and location 2026; Census 2021; Consumer Prices Index
